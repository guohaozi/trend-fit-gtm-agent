# Worked Examples — three gold-standard demo cases

These show the target depth and tone for a GTM Brief. They are the **single source of
truth** for the three demo cases — the score tables here match `data/*.json` and
`outputs/*_report.md` exactly (Fashion 90 · Robotics 74 · AI tool 89). All scores use
only the legal anchors {0, 25, 50, 75, 100}; the *reasoning* is the point.

> All three are **evidence-light** examples (no live metrics supplied), so every score
> is justified qualitatively from the profile + trend, with assumptions labelled. In a
> real run, plug in `competitor-evidence` findings to harden the numbers.

---

## Example 1 — Apparel × "quiet luxury / old money outfit"

**Product:** Mid-range men's clothing brand · US market · 20–35 male young
professionals · clean fit, comfortable basics, affordable premium look · brand tone:
tasteful/practical · goal: awareness + sales · risk tolerance: medium.
**Trend:** "quiet luxury / old money outfit" (TikTok/Reels, US, styling trend toward
understated, expensive-*looking* classic menswear).

**Scoring**

| Dimension | Wt | Score | Wtd | Why |
|-----------|----|-------|-----|-----|
| Audience Overlap | 20% | 100 | 20.0 | Trend audience = style-conscious 20–35 US men — exactly the ICP. |
| Use-case Relevance | 20% | 100 | 20.0 | Brand's "clean, premium-looking basics" *is* the quiet-luxury look. |
| Message Bridge | 15% | 100 | 15.0 | One honest line: "look expensive without luxury prices." |
| Creative Feasibility | 15% | 100 | 15.0 | Outfit/styling UGC is the cheapest, most repeatable format with existing product. |
| Commercial Intent | 10% | 75 | 7.5 | Outfit trends drive "where to buy" shopping behavior. |
| Brand Safety | 10% | 50 | 5.0 | Classism risk: "look rich" framing can alienate/insult the audience. |
| Timing & Saturation | 10% | 75 | 7.5 | Still active; needs the affordability angle to differentiate. |
| **Total** | | | **90** | → **Strong Go** (lead with classism guardrails) |

**Recommendation:** Strong Go (raw 90.00 → 90). The fit is near-perfect; the *only* real
job is to avoid sounding classist.

- **Angle:** "Look expensive without luxury prices." Alternates: "Quiet luxury on a
  real budget"; "The $40 piece that reads $400."
- **Risk:** Medium — classism (Medium): "be rich/elite" framing alienates the
  price-conscious base and invites "fake rich" mockery. Mitigate by selling the
  *look*, not the *class*. Cultural-erasure (Low) if presented as universal taste.
- **Voice:** Tasteful, practical, confident, not arrogant.
- **Use:** clean, polished, timeless, premium-looking, effortless, everyday.
- **Avoid:** rich, fake luxury, elite, old-money cosplay, "poor man's luxury".
- **KOL:** men's fashion micro-creators, office-outfit creators, lifestyle creators
  (mid-tier; their audience trusts "affordable but sharp" recs).

## Example 2 — Home robotics × "AI home gadgets / smart home setup"

**Product:** Home robotics brand · US + Japan · smart-home users, families, pet owners
· reliable home-assistant robot that makes daily routines easier · risk tolerance:
medium.
**Trend:** "AI home gadgets / smart home setup" (setup tours, "gadgets that changed my
home" videos).

**Scoring**

| Dimension | Wt | Score | Wtd | Why |
|-----------|----|-------|-----|-----|
| Audience Overlap | 20% | 75 | 15.0 | Smart-home/family audience overlaps ICP well; not all are buyers. |
| Use-case Relevance | 20% | 100 | 20.0 | The product is literally a smart-home gadget — native to the trend. |
| Message Bridge | 15% | 75 | 11.25 | "A robot that actually helps with the daily routine" — clean bridge. |
| Creative Feasibility | 15% | 50 | 7.5 | Needs real working demos; comments scrutinize "does it work?". |
| Commercial Intent | 10% | 75 | 7.5 | Considered purchase; people research smart-home gear before buying. |
| Brand Safety | 10% | 50 | 5.0 | Privacy + over-promising risks are real for home robots. |
| Timing & Saturation | 10% | 75 | 7.5 | Smart-home content is evergreen-growing; room with a trust angle. |
| **Total** | | | **74** | → **Go** · qualifier: trust-building angle (raw 73.75 → 74) |

**Recommendation:** Go with a trust-building angle. The fit is strong; the swing risks
are privacy and over-promising, so the campaign must *show*, not claim.

- **Angle:** "I let a robot help manage my home for 7 days." Alternates: "Real life
  with a home robot (the honest version)"; "What a home robot actually does for a busy
  family."
- **Risk:** Medium — privacy/surveillance (Medium): cameras/sensors trigger "is it
  watching me?"; over-promising (Medium): implying full autonomy invites backlash and
  is a legal risk; gimmick perception (Low). Mitigate with real footage, user-control
  framing, honest limitations.
- **Voice:** Warm, trustworthy, practical, slightly futuristic.
- **Use:** helpful, reliable, daily routine, family-friendly, simple setup, you stay in
  control.
- **Avoid:** replace humans, fully autonomous life, surveillance, sci-fi butler.
- **KOL:** smart-home reviewers, family-lifestyle creators, pet owners, tech YouTubers
  (reviewers add credibility hardware needs).

## Example 3 — AI photo tool × "AI profile photo / product photo before-after"

**Product:** AI photo-editing tool · US/Japan/SEA · creators, photographers,
e-commerce sellers · fast, high-quality AI editing for social + product photos · risk
tolerance: medium.
**Trend:** "AI profile photo / product photo before-after" (before/after reveals).

**Scoring**

| Dimension | Wt | Score | Wtd | Why |
|-----------|----|-------|-----|-----|
| Audience Overlap | 20% | 100 | 20.0 | Creators/sellers/photographers = exact ICP and exact trend audience. |
| Use-case Relevance | 20% | 100 | 20.0 | The tool *produces* the before/after — perfectly native. |
| Message Bridge | 15% | 75 | 11.25 | "Turn raw photos into scroll-stopping visuals in seconds." |
| Creative Feasibility | 15% | 100 | 15.0 | Before/after is the cheapest, most repeatable format for this tool. |
| Commercial Intent | 10% | 100 | 10.0 | Before/after of a paid editing result + "link in bio" is a transactional trend. |
| Brand Safety | 10% | 75 | 7.5 | Manageable; main risk is fake-identity/unrealistic-beauty framing. |
| Timing & Saturation | 10% | 50 | 5.0 | AI-edit content is crowded; needs a workflow/speed differentiator. |
| **Total** | | | **89** | → **Strong Go** (raw 88.75 → 89) |

**Recommendation:** Strong Go. Native format, exact audience, trivial to produce. Keep
it honest (real, repeatable results) and differentiate on speed/workflow vs. the
crowded field.

- **Angle:** "Turn raw photos into scroll-stopping visuals in seconds." Alternates:
  "Product photos that actually sell — in one tap"; "My 10-second editing workflow."
- **Risk:** Medium-low — deception/fake-identity (Low–Medium): avoid "fake face"/
  identity-swap framing; over-promising (Low): don't imply impossible transformations.
  Mitigate by showing realistic, reproducible results on real user photos.
- **Voice:** Creative, practical, maker-oriented.
- **Use:** before-after, creator workflow, product-ready, edit faster, visual upgrade.
- **Avoid:** perfect face, fake identity, effortless wealth, unrealistic transformation.
- **KOL:** AI-tool reviewers, e-commerce creators, photographers, design educators.

## What these examples demonstrate
- Every score has a one-line, profile-grounded reason — no orphan numbers.
- Brand Safety honestly held the apparel/robotics cases at 50, shaping the whole brief.
- Words-to-avoid map 1:1 to the risks named.
- Angles are filmable lines, not slogans.
- No fabricated metrics anywhere — all reasoning is qualitative and labelled.
