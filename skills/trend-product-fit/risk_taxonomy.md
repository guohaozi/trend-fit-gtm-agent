# Risk Taxonomy — how to assess and phrase brand risk

Use these named categories in section 6 of the GTM Brief. For each *real* risk (don't
list categories that don't apply), give: **category → severity (Low/Medium/High) →
what triggers it → mitigation**. This keeps the risk section specific instead of
boilerplate, and it feeds directly into the Words-to-avoid list.

Severity feeds the **Brand Safety** dimension score and the override rules in
`SKILL.md` (Brand Safety ≤ 25 caps the recommendation; low risk tolerance + < 50 →
No-go).

---

## Risk categories

### 1. Classism / wealth-signalling
The trend celebrates wealth, status, or "looking rich". Risk: alienating the actual
(price-sensitive) audience, or sounding like you're mocking them.
- Triggers: "old money", "quiet luxury", "rich aesthetic", luxury-cosplay framing.
- Mitigation: sell the *look/outcome* ("looks expensive", "polished") not the *class*
  ("be rich", "elite"). Center accessibility, not exclusion.

### 2. Vulgarity / NSFW / shock
The trend's attention comes from crude, sexual, or shock content.
- Triggers: suggestive challenges, gross-out formats, profanity-led humor.
- Mitigation: usually don't ride it; if you must, take the trend's *structure* without
  its shock content. Often a Brand Safety ≤ 25.

### 3. Political / cultural / values
The trend touches politics, religion, identity, or contested cultural ground.
- Triggers: protest hashtags, nationalist framing, culture-war bait, religious moments.
- Mitigation: stay out unless the brand has a genuine, owned stance. Neutrality that
  reads as opportunistic is its own risk.

### 4. Cultural appropriation / erasure
The aesthetic borrows from a culture/community without credit, or erases its origin.
- Triggers: "clean girl", borrowed slang, sacred symbols as decoration.
- Mitigation: credit origins, don't strip meaning, avoid costume-ing a culture.

### 5. Over-promising / false claims (esp. hardware & AI)
The trend invites exaggeration the product can't honestly back.
- Triggers: "fully autonomous", "replaces humans", "perfect every time", "effortless
  wealth", before/afters the product can't reliably reproduce.
- Mitigation: show real, repeatable results; caption the conditions; never imply an
  outcome you can't deliver. This is also a *legal* (false-advertising) risk.

### 6. Privacy / surveillance (esp. smart home, AI, data products)
The trend or the product raises "is it watching/listening to me?" fears.
- Triggers: cameras, always-on mics, data capture, "it knows everything about you".
- Mitigation: lead with user control, on-device/private framing, opt-in language.

### 7. Deception / fake identity (esp. AI media tools)
The output could be used to fake a person, result, or reality.
- Triggers: "fake your face", "look like someone else", undetectable edits, fake reviews.
- Mitigation: frame as enhancement/workflow, not deception; avoid identity-swap angles.

### 8. Health / safety / sensitive claims
The trend touches health, money, mental health, weight, or other regulated/sensitive
areas.
- Triggers: weight-loss, financial returns, medical outcomes, "cures", body transformation.
- Mitigation: avoid outcome promises; add disclaimers; often legal review required.

### 9. Cringe / forced fit (the quiet killer)
No legal/ethical problem — the brand just looks try-hard riding something that isn't
its audience. Most damaging for serious/B2B brands.
- Triggers: a corporate account doing a teen dance; jargon mixed with slang; jumping a
  trend a week after it peaked.
- Mitigation: this usually means the *fit score itself is low* — reflect it in
  Use-case/Creative, not just here. Sometimes the right call is No-go.

### 10. Saturation / me-too
Not unsafe, but every competitor already did it → you look derivative and late.
- Triggers: trend is mainstream, your category is crowded in it.
- Mitigation: only enter with a genuinely differentiated angle, or skip.

### 11. Platform / legal / IP
Copyrighted audio, brand IP, regulated-category ad rules, FTC disclosure.
- Triggers: trending songs you can't license for ads, using another brand's IP,
  undisclosed paid partnerships.
- Mitigation: use licensed/commercial audio for paid, disclose partnerships, clear IP.

## How to write the risk section (template)

> **Risk: Medium.** The "quiet luxury" trend carries **classism risk (Medium)** —
> framing the brand as a way to "look rich" can alienate the price-conscious 20–35
> audience and invite "fake rich" mockery. *Mitigation:* sell the polished, expensive
> *look* at an honest price; never claim status. Secondary **cultural-erasure risk
> (Low)** if the aesthetic is presented as universal. No legal exposure.

Keep it to the risks that actually apply. A brief that lists eleven generic risks is as
useless as one that lists none.
