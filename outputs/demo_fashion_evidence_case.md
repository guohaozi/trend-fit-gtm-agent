## 1. Evidence blocks

### Finding A — Trend trajectory (feeds Timing & Saturation)
- **Origin & peak:** The trend "gained momentum in early 2023" off HBO's *Succession*
  S4 (premiere 26 Mar 2023), Gwyneth Paltrow's trial outfits, and Sofia Richie's
  wedding; "quiet luxury" surpassed **35 billion views on TikTok**. [Refinery29]
- **Decline:** Multiple 2025 analyses describe the *hype* as past its peak and evolving
  rather than rising — "while the peak of 'quiet luxury' has passed, its influence
  continues," shifting toward "subtle statement" dressing and niche categories (e.g.
  quiet-luxury handbags). [Accio trend analysis; Influencers Time]
- **Read:** As of mid-2026 this is a **mature, post-peak** aesthetic, not an early/rising
  trend. Entering now is "me-too" unless differentiated.
- *Confidence:* Origin/peak = high (mainstream press). Exact decline timing = medium
  (secondary trend-analysis sites, not raw Google Trends export). Treated qualitatively,
  no fabricated percentages.

### Finding B — Documented brand-safety backlash (feeds Brand Safety)
- **Classism, on record:** *"Now, people say outright that they're going for an 'old
  money' aesthetic, which is clearly classist."* — Jonathan Square, Parsons School of
  Design, quoted in [Refinery29].
- **Racial/cultural critique, on record:** *"There is a racist undertone in this because
  it goes against the native cultural expressions of other communities, who are not the
  white elite."* — Laura Beltrán-Rubio, Culturas de Moda, in [Refinery29]. A dedicated
  [Essence] piece is titled *"Quiet Luxury And Its Racist Implications."*
- **Tone-deafness:** Critics call stealth-wealth signalling "tone-deaf" in an uncertain
  economy; "quiet luxury is actually a loud statement about class." [multiple op-eds]
- **Read:** The classism/racism/tone-deaf risk is **not hypothetical** — it is a
  recurring, named-source critique in major outlets. This is exactly the reputational
  landmine the Brand Safety dimension exists to catch.
- *Confidence:* High — verbatim, attributed quotes from named experts in mainstream press.

### Finding C — Mid-market competitor activity (feeds Use-case Relevance, Commercial Intent, Saturation)
- **An entire "affordable quiet luxury" sub-genre exists**, with mid-range brands named
  repeatedly: **Uniqlo, COS, Mango, Massimo Dutti** ("looks 'quiet luxury' on a
  Tuesday"). [The VOU; Chic Style Collective]
- **Saturation signal:** the affordable angle is itself crowded — listicles like *"13
  Affordable Quiet Luxury Brands…Under £100"* and *"12 The Row dupes"* are abundant.
  [Chic Style Collective; The Nod Mag]
- **The angle is market-validated:** consumer framing is literally *"choosing items that
  are well-made, versatile, and understated… anyone can achieve this on a budget"* and
  *"quiet luxury is all about intention, not price"* [The VOU] — i.e. the demo's
  campaign angle ("look expensive without luxury prices") is exactly what is already
  working.
- *Confidence:* High for "the sub-genre and named brands exist"; medium for relative
  saturation (directional, not a measured share-of-voice).

## 2. Evidence synthesis for the decision
- **Saturation read:** mid-market participation is heavy and the affordable angle is
  crowded → **lowers Timing**, and means we must differentiate, not just enter.
- **Audience / use-case confirmation:** named mid-range competitors prove the product
  type belongs natively → **confirms Use-case Relevance = 100**.
- **Winning angle observed:** "expensive look, honest price / intention not price" is
  the live, working frame → **confirms the demo's campaign angle**.
- **Risk evidence:** named-expert classism quotes (Refinery29) plus a racist-implications
  critique (Essence) →
  **confirms Brand Safety = 50 and makes the words-to-avoid list non-negotiable.**
- **White space:** an explicitly **anti-classist, accessibility-first** take ("the look
  is for everyone, the price proves it") is the differentiated lane most affordable
  dupe-listicles don't own.

## 4. What the evidence changes in the brief
- **Recommendation:** still Strong Go, but reframed — *"Go, but you are late; win on a
  differentiated anti-classist angle, not on being first."*
- **Risk section:** upgrade classism from "a risk to watch" to "a documented backlash" —
  cite that critics call the aesthetic classist/racist, so the affordability-and-access
  framing is mandatory, not optional.
- **Timing guidance:** add "the affordable-dupe space is saturated; differentiate or
  skip the generic version."
- **Words-to-avoid:** evidence reinforces banning *old money* (as identity), *rich*,
  *elite* — these are the exact words the cited critiques attack.
